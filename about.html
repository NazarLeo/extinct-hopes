What I makePosted 4:58 AM

One facility, two survivors. The prequel follows a maintenance worker on the night shift, back when the lab still worked. Chapters 1 and 2 follow the same person — the one who fell through the floor and never made it out on the first try. Each chapter stands on its own, but played in order they tell a single story.

The rule across every chapter is the same: no fixed camera views and no scares on a timer. You walk the corridors yourself, and the thing down there moves on its own schedule. If it catches you, it is because it learned your route — not because a script was waiting for you to reach a trigger.

InfluencesPosted 5:00 AM
five nights at freddy's, alien: isolation, outlast, amnesia, scp — containment breach, dead space, cry of fear, soviet brutalism, analog horror tapes
Rules I don't breakPosted 5:02 AM
  • You walk, always. Every corridor you learn is one you can use later — and so can it.
  • The enemy hunts. It patrols on its own schedule and learns your route. Hiding works only if you got there before it did.
  • The place tells the story. Notes, recordings and the state of the rooms carry the plot.
  • It has to run on real phones. A horror game that stutters is not scary, it is annoying.
